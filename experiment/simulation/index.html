📐 Documented Mathematical Model Simplified Physics
Photodiode Current (I_PD):

I_PD = ℛ_λ · P_trans + I_ambient + I_dark + I_noise

Where ℛ_660 = 0.45 A/W and ℛ_940 = 0.65 A/W are silicon PIN quantum responsivities.

Transimpedance Voltage (V_TIA):

V_TIA = I_PD · R_f (Clamped: 0.0V ≤ V ≤ 3.3V)

Op-amp output rails saturate if optical power or feedback resistor R_f is driven too high.

Synchronous Ambient Cancellation:

V_demux = V_sample - V_dark

Subtracts stray room lighting captured during the dark interleaving clock phase.

ADC Quantization:

V_ADC = round(V_in / V_ref · (2^N - 1)) · ΔV

Demonstrates quantization noise when lowering bit-depth to 8-bit.

📖 About the Project Biomedical Instrumentation

The SpO₂ Circuit Emulation and Calibration 3D Virtual Laboratory is an open-source educational platform designed for biomedical engineering students, electrical engineers, and medical instrumentation researchers. It provides an intuitive, hands-on environment to understand non-invasive pulse oximetry principles from the photophysics of hemoglobin to transistor-level signal conditioning and clinical calibration curves.

🔬 Beer-Lambert Law & Dual-Wavelength Photophysics Fundamental Theory

Transmission pulse oximetry relies on the differential optical absorption of oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO₂) and deoxygenated hemoglobin (Hb) across two specific electromagnetic wavelengths:

🔴 Red Channel (Nominal 660 nm)

Deoxygenated hemoglobin (Hb) absorbs roughly 10 times more light at 660 nm (ε_Hb = 0.810) than oxygenated hemoglobin (ε_HbO₂ = 0.080). Consequently, as blood desaturates, Red light attenuation increases markedly.

🔵 Infrared Channel (Nominal 940 nm)

Oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO₂) exhibits higher optical absorption at 940 nm (ε_HbO₂ = 0.290) than deoxygenated hemoglobin (ε_Hb = 0.180). This wavelength provides a reliable reference for total pulsatile blood volume.

Beer-Lambert Equation: I(t) = I₀ · 10^(-[ε_Hb·C_Hb + ε_HbO₂·C_HbO₂]·L(t))
Modulation Ratio: R = [(AC_660 / DC_660) / (AC_940 / DC_940)]
⚡ Electronic Signal Conditioning Architecture Hardware Design
Transimpedance Pre-Amplifier (TIA):

Photodiodes operate in photovoltaic or photoconductive modes producing microamperes. A high-gain op-amp with feedback resistor R_f (50 kΩ - 1 MΩ) and compensation capacitor C_f translates this current into a measurable voltage.

Synchronous Demodulation:

Stray room lighting (such as 50Hz/60Hz fluorescent hum or sunlight) is subtracted by pulsing the LEDs alternately and capturing dark-phase baseline noise.

Active Bandpass Filtering:

A 1st-order active high-pass filter (fc ≈ 0.5 Hz) eliminates large DC tissue offsets without phase-distorting the pulse wave, followed by a 2nd-order Sallen-Key low-pass filter (fc ≈ 8 Hz) for anti-aliasing.

Analog-to-Digital Conversion:

A 12-bit SAR ADC samples the AC PPG signal at 500 Hz, achieving sufficient dynamic range to resolve perfusion indices down to 0.2%.

⚠️ Educational Disclaimer & Clinical Caveats Important Notice

This virtual laboratory is strictly an educational simulation tool developed for engineering pedagogy. It is NOT a certified medical device and must never be utilized for patient diagnosis, therapeutic decisions, or medical monitoring.

Real-world pulse oximetry is subject to limitations including motion artifacts, hypoperfusion, severe vasoconstriction, abnormal hemoglobins (carboxyhemoglobin, methemoglobin), and ambient optical interference that are modeled here in simplified mathematical form.
